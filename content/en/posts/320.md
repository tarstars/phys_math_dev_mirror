Or is it too late?

I came across a line in a chat. It went something like “An LLM proved the Riemann hypothesis, and humans didn’t.”

My first thought was, one LLM? I’d bet on a swarm of agents working together.

My next thought was, isn’t that what mathematicians already do? We have journals. We talk to each other. We build on each other’s results. So where’s the difference?

Maybe it’s the incentives. You can give a whole swarm of agents a common goal. Humans applying for grants have good reason to carve out a little niche of their own. Do we end up playing a game of “share enough to build a reputation, but keep enough back to stay indispensable”?

Or maybe it’s ambition. When I say “I want to see this Millennium Prize Problem solved,” how much of what I mean is “I want to be the one who solves it”?

Could we build a space where helping someone else finish a proof is worth as much as finishing it yourself? What could we do for math, physics, or biology if more of us could afford to work that way?

We even have a common rival to bring us together now.

Or is it too late?
