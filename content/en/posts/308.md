# Efficient calculation of nth row of Pascal's triangle

Finally, let's gather all the stones we've thrown. I [mentioned](<https://t.me/phys_math_dev/304>) calculating the whole n-th row in Pascal's triangle in T=O(n), M=O(1). There was also [leetcode 62](<https://t.me/phys_math_dev/302>), a problem about the number of paths a robot can take through a rectangular grid. [I explained](<https://t.me/phys_math_dev/306>) how to skew Pascal's triangle into that rectangular grid and [showed](<https://t.me/phys_math_dev/307>) what the recurrence relation between its elements looks like.

There are four ways to define the numbers that live in Pascal's triangle. It's a beautiful crossroads, and I hope that one day I'll write about it.

In the previous posts, we discussed two out of four: $C_n^k$ is the number of ways to place k indistinguishable objects in a row of n cells, with at most one object per cell; it is also the kth element in the nth row of Pascal's triangle. To solve the problem, we'll use the third definition:

$C_n^k = \frac {n!} {k!(n-k)!}$

Let's take one step along the nth row. Unexpected question: what is the ratio between two neighbors, $C_n^k$ and $C_n^{k+1}$ ? The math is quite simple:

$$
\frac{C_n^{k+1}}{C_n^k} = \frac{\frac{n!}{(k+1)!(n-k-1)!}}{\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}} = \frac{n!}{(k+1)!(n-k-1)!} \cdot \frac{k!(n-k)!}{n!} = \frac{k!}{(k+1)!} \cdot \frac{(n-k)!}{(n-k-1)!} = \frac{n-k}{k+1}
$$

In this math you strike out $k!$ in numerator with factorial sign in denominator. Funny.

Taking all this into account, the program [from that post](<https://t.me/phys_math_dev/302>) becomes trivial. Ta-da!!!
